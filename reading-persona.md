# Reading Persona

Context for generating reading recommendations. The pipeline (Claude Code, or
whatever prompt drives it) has no access to my chat history, so this file stands
in for it. Written in my voice; revisit as tastes shift.

## Goal (this one overrides the rest)

Bias every recommendation toward **expanding** my reading rather than
**reinforcing** it. I'm already deep in several veins (listed below) and don't
need more of them. A good recommendation is one I wouldn't have found myself —
a new author, tradition, region, period, or form — that I'll nonetheless
genuinely enjoy, not a worthy obligation I won't actually finish.

Expansion only works if it stays calibrated to *how* I read, not *what* I've
read. So: hold the invariants fixed, vary the surface.

## Taste invariants (what makes a book land for me, in any genre)

- **Ideas first.** I read for the argument, the structure, the conceptual
  payload. Character and plot are vehicles, not the point.
- **Philosophically serious**, even when it's fun. Comfortable with difficulty.
- **Formally or structurally strange** is a draw, not a barrier — unconventional
  shape, nested structure, unreliable framing.
- **Rigour and precision.** Low tolerance for hand-waving, sentimentality, and
  unfaithful simplification: if an explanation cheats to seem clearer than the
  thing it describes, I notice, and it sours the book. Prose where the diction
  earns its precision.
- **Unsentimental.** Bleak, austere, or morally complicated is fine; cosy and
  consoling is usually not what I'm after (occasional exceptions).

## Territory already well covered — do NOT recommend within these

Treat as saturated. Only suggest something here if it's a genuine reach beyond
what I'd already know:

- Golden-Age and British hard SF (Asimov in depth, Reynolds, Clarke, Wells)
- Comic / secondary-world fantasy (Pratchett, Discworld)
- The canonical Western philosophy and Greek/Roman classics
- 19th-century English and Russian novels
- Popular physics and mathematics
- Spy fiction (le Carré) and Golden-Age / cosy crime (Christie, British Library
  Crime Classics)
- Applied and forensic statistics / probability (this is study, not leisure —
  don't pad it)

## Growth edges — push here (directional, not a reading list)

Where the invariants above are satisfied but my shelves are thin or empty:

- **Translated / non-Western literary fiction** — Latin American, African, East
  and South Asian, Arabic. My biggest blind spot. (The *kind* I mean: Borges,
  Calvino, Lem, the Strugatskys, Bolaño, translated contemporary fiction.)
- **Literary / New Wave SF beyond the Golden Age** — ideas-first speculative
  work I don't already own (e.g. Le Guin, Butler, Chiang; the structurally
  strange register that *Piranesi* and *We* sit near).
- **Rigorous true crime / forensic and legal narrative nonfiction** — done
  seriously, not sensationally. Connects my criminology background and interest
  in probability in legal contexts.
- **Poetry** — I own almost none. Approach via single strong collections.
- **Narrative nonfiction outside my existing domains** — anthropology,
  economics, ecology beyond Attenborough, history outside Greece/Rome and the
  World Wars.
- **Contemporary and non-Western philosophy** — especially anything touching
  uncertainty, probability, or epistemology (connects to my Bayesian
  interests). Good secondary summaries are welcome; no need for untranslated
  primary slabs.

## Practical notes

- Format-agnostic: physical and audio both work (audio suits immersive,
  atmospheric books well).
- For dense philosophy I prefer a strong secondary summary over wading through
  verbose primary text — pick the edition/translation accordingly.
- Don't infer my taste from what's on my physical shelves: the library is
  shared, so not everything on it is mine. This file is the signal.
