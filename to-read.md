### 2026-W23

- **The Hour of the Star** — Clarice Lispector (1977). A Brazilian
  modernist's last book: an uneducated migrant's life narrated by a
  self-doubting alter ego who keeps interrupting to question his own
  fiction. Structurally strange, philosophically serious, unsentimental —
  and squarely in the translated, non-Western literary fiction you said is
  your biggest blind spot.
- **Stories of Your Life and Others** — Ted Chiang (2002). Eight stories
  that are almost pure conceptual payload: a language that rewrites how its
  speakers experience time, a proof that mathematics is inconsistent, free
  will under determinism. Ideas-first speculative fiction beyond the
  Golden Age, exactly the register *Piranesi* and *We* sit near.
- **Autobiography of Red** — Anne Carson (1998). A verse novel that
  retells the Greek myth of Geryon and Herakles as a contemporary
  coming-of-age story. Carson is a classicist, so the rigour is real and
  the form is genuinely strange — a way into poetry, which you own almost
  none of, without leaving the invariants behind.
