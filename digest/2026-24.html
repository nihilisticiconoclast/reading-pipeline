Week 2026-24

Recommendations for the week of 8 June 2026.

This week targets three growth edges untouched last week: forensic and legal narrative nonfiction, history of medicine as ideas-driven narrative, and contemporary philosophy of epistemology. The connecting thread is how knowledge is built, challenged, and revised under pressure — in the IRA's enforced silence, in oncology's failures and conceptual breakthroughs, and in the logic of mathematical proof. Each satisfies the taste invariants (ideas first, rigorous, unsentimental) while reaching into shelves that are currently thin or empty.

Highbrow

Say Nothing: A True Story of Murder and Memory in Northern Ireland — Patrick Radden Keefe (2018)

Political history of the Troubles framed around a single 1972 IRA murder — told through court records, deathbed confessions, and the Boston College oral history archive. It operates at the intersection of forensic rigour and moral ambiguity that the persona's criminology background connects with directly; forensic where most true crime is sensational. National Book Critics Circle Award for Nonfiction.

The Emperor of All Maladies: A Biography of Cancer — Siddhartha Mukherjee (2010)

History of cancer research told as a detective story about conceptual shifts rather than lucky discoveries — from mustard-gas chemotherapy to targeted therapy, each breakthrough resting on a revised theory of what cancer is. Ideas-first narrative nonfiction; the "biography of a disease" framing is genuinely structurally novel. Pulitzer Prize for General Nonfiction.

Proofs and Refutations: The Logic of Mathematical Discovery — Imre Lakatos (1976)

A Socratic dialogue set in an imaginary classroom where teacher and students spend the whole book arguing about Euler's formula for polyhedra — each "proof" dismantled by a counterexample, each counterexample met by a refined definition. The argument is that mathematics is a quasi-empirical process of conjecture and revision: structurally strange, philosophically serious, and directly mapped onto Bayesian interests in how beliefs are revised under new evidence.
