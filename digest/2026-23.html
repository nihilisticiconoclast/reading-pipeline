Week 2026-23

Recommendations for the week of 1 June 2026.

This week pushes on three growth edges at once: translated non-Western literary fiction, ideas-first SF beyond the Golden Age, and poetry. Each holds the invariants — ideas first, formally strange, rigorous, unsentimental — while reaching into shelves that are currently thin or empty.

Highbrow

The Hour of the Star — Clarice Lispector (1977)

A Brazilian modernist's last book: an uneducated migrant's life narrated by a self-doubting alter ego who keeps interrupting to question his own fiction. Structurally strange, philosophically serious, unsentimental — and squarely in the translated, non-Western literary fiction that's your biggest blind spot.

Stories of Your Life and Others — Ted Chiang (2002)

Eight stories that are almost pure conceptual payload: a language that rewrites how its speakers experience time, a proof that mathematics is inconsistent, free will under determinism. Ideas-first speculative fiction beyond the Golden Age, exactly the register Piranesi and We sit near.

Autobiography of Red — Anne Carson (1998)

A verse novel that retells the Greek myth of Geryon and Herakles as a contemporary coming-of-age story. Carson is a classicist, so the rigour is real and the form is genuinely strange — a way into poetry, which you own almost none of, without leaving the invariants behind.
